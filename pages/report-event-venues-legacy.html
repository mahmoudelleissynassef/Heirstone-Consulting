Event Venues and Legacy: Planning for day two

What became of 30 Olympic, Expo and World Cup sites after the crowds left, and what the Gulf and Egypt can settle now for the venues they are building next.

In brief

The day after an event is decided years before it opens.

We followed 30 Olympic, Expo and World Cup sites from closing ceremony to October 2026. The sites that reopened quickly had their landowner and their buyer or tenant fixed in contracts before the event. Where these were settled afterwards, the main reuse took between three and 22 years, and some sites are still waiting.

  1. Contracts made before the event set the pace of reuse.

    Lisbon's Expo site reopened as a city district 15 days after the fair closed, Expo City Dubai after six months and part of London's Olympic Park within a year. Where the anchor or development partner came later, the main reuse took three to 22 years.

  2. Seats are the hardest asset to carry into day two.

    London kept 16% of its Aquatics Centre seats by unbolting two temporary wings. Qatar planned to remove 58% of the seats in its seven new stadiums; by December 2025 we found no confirmed removal.

  3. Homes are the most dependable legacy.

    Athletes' villages convert because a flat still has a buyer once the event has gone. Paris is converting its village into 2,807 homes, Tokyo's Harumi Flag holds 5,632, and London sold its village for £825m before the Games.

  4. District legacies take decades, and the cost line comes first.

    Sevilla TechPark reports 35,806 jobs on the Expo 92 site, 33 years on. Every Olympic Games with data has overrun its budget, by 156% on average in real terms.

  5. The Gulf's pipeline is large and still early enough to test.

    Saudi Arabia's ten new 2034 stadiums add 508,505 seats, against Saudi Pro League average crowds of about 8,000. Egypt already holds about 255,000 seats in its three largest stadiums; its day-two task is to find them tenants and a calendar.

What it means

Recommendations by audience

Governments and land agencies

Write the day-two owner, tenant and seat count into the host agreement, with a published reopening date.

Master developers

Treat the village and the event estate as the first phase of a district and phase the rest on absorption measured in decades.

Investors and lenders

Underwrite households and anchor leases, and treat seat revenue as event-day income.

Frequently asked questions

What happens to Olympic, Expo and World Cup venues after the event?

It depends on contracts signed before the event. Where the landowner and the buyer or tenant were fixed in advance, sites reopened within a year; where they were settled afterwards, the main reuse took three to 22 years.

What is the most reliable legacy of a mega-event?

Housing. Athletes' villages convert because homes still have buyers once the event has gone: Paris is converting its village into 2,807 homes and Tokyo's Harumi Flag holds 5,632.

What should Saudi Arabia and the Gulf settle now for their event venues?

The day-two owner, tenant and seat count, written into the host agreement with a published reopening date. Saudi Arabia's ten new 2034 stadiums add 508,505 seats against league crowds of about 8,000.
