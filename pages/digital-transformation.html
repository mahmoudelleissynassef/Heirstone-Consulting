Digital Transformation

Services, digitised end to end.
We help governments digitise the services that real estate and construction depend on — permitting, land registration, licensing and asset data — so that capital moves at the speed of the decision, not the queue.

In most of the markets where Heirstone works, the binding constraint on real estate and construction investment is not capital or demand. It is the time and uncertainty between a decision to invest and a permit in hand, a title registered, or an asset lawfully occupied. Digital transformation of those services is therefore not an IT programme — it is economic infrastructure.

Services, digitised end to end

A developer experiences the state as a single sequence: acquire land, prove title, obtain a permit, build under inspection, register and occupy. Government experiences it as separate mandates across several institutions. Reform that only digitises each mandate in isolation leaves the sequence exactly as long as it was. Our work begins from the sequence, not the org chart.

Our approach

  • Diagnose the journey, not the department — timed end to end, using real application volumes rather than published service standards.
  • Simplify before digitising — remove duplicated approvals and introduce risk-based review tracks so the process is worth automating.
  • Specify functionally, procure independently — architecture and requirements defined so the authority can buy well, with no vendor allegiance on our side.
  • Integrate the registers — cadastre, land registry, identity, utilities and payment, so evidence is presented once.
  • Build the institution around the platform — operating model, capability and published service standards, or the gains erode within a year.

What we deliver

  • Permitting and construction-licensing reform and digitisation strategies
  • Land registry and property transaction modernisation programmes
  • Government service design, service standards and channel strategy
  • National BIM mandates, asset information requirements and digital twin strategy
  • Functional architecture, procurement strategy and phased rollout plans

Frequently asked questions

What is digital transformation in the real estate and construction sectors?

It is the redesign and digitisation of the government services that property and construction activity depends on — building permits and construction licensing, land registration and property transfer, compliance and inspection, and the asset data generated by public construction. The objective is not a portal but a shorter, more predictable path from investment decision to delivered asset, with a reliable public record behind it.

Do you build the technology, or advise on it?

We advise. Our work covers the diagnostic, the process and regulatory reform, the target service design, the functional architecture and the procurement and rollout strategy — the decisions that determine whether a platform investment succeeds. We are deliberately independent of any vendor or platform, and we work alongside the systems integrators that build and operate the solution.

Why does digitisation so often fail to reduce approval times?

Because the underlying process is usually digitised rather than redesigned. If four departments each require the same document and review sequentially, an online form simply moves that sequence onto a screen. Meaningful reduction comes from removing duplicated approvals, introducing risk-based review tracks, and integrating the registers that hold the evidence — which is why we treat regulatory simplification as a precondition rather than a later phase.
