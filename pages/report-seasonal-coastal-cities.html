Seasonal Coastal Cities: Built for August

Why resort coasts empty out after the summer, what has made a few of them work all year, and what it means for Egypt's North Coast, Ras El Hekma and the Saudi Red Sea.

In brief

A resort coast's winter is largely settled before its first home is sold.

We measured seasonality at 20 resort airports and in 12 coastal regions, and the housing of 31 resort towns. The coasts that work all year have two things the others lack: homes that are lived in and a winter market that arrives by air. Most also have a large employer that stays open in February.

  1. Airport counts understate how seasonal a resort economy is.

    Our Seasonality Index divides a year's air passengers by those of the busiest month: Mykonos's year is worth 4.0 peak months and Lanzarote's 10.8. Crete's tourist nights in August 2025 were 78.6 times January's, and its bed-places were 94% used in August and 1.2% in January.

  2. Winter demand comes from abroad, and that is where growth has been.

    Foreign guests supply 80.9% of the Algarve's November to March nights. Between 2014 and 2024 Faro's winter passengers rose 98.6%, twice the growth of its summer, and Malaga's January traffic rose 128%.

  3. The housing mix a resort starts with tends to stay.

    La Grande-Motte had 2.47 dwellings per resident in 1975 and 2.46 in 2023. Of 11 French resorts, only the two inside an urban corridor have a majority of homes lived in all year.

  4. Few anchors move January, and the popular ones are small.

    Large universities, state-funded winter travel and a big city within daily reach go with fuller winters. Spain's 27,875 digital-nomad permit holders equal about a ninth of Marbella's estimated summer influx.

  5. Egypt's North Coast is being built at a scale beyond any resort in our benchmark, on a summer market.

    Matrouh had Egypt's highest housing vacancy in the 2017 census, 46.9%. Ras El Hekma is planned for over one million people, 1.8 times Matrouh's population, while New Alamein's year-round residents are estimated at 13,000 to 15,000.

What it means

Recommendations by audience

Governments and land agencies

Fix the housing mix, the first institutions and the phasing of utilities in the masterplan, and publish monthly data on airports, hotels and utilities.

Master developers

Report January occupancy alongside sales and lead each phase with hotels, campuses or clinics that stay open all year.

Investors and lenders

Underwrite on the November to March share of demand and treat incentive-led and event-led demand as cyclical.

Frequently asked questions

Why do resort coasts empty out after the summer?

Because most were built for a domestic summer market: homes are second homes rather than lived in, and winter demand, which comes mainly from abroad by air, was never planned for. Crete's tourist nights in August 2025 were 78.6 times January's.

What makes a coastal city work all year?

Homes that are lived in, a winter market that arrives by air and, usually, a large employer such as a university or hospital that stays open in February. The housing mix a resort starts with tends to persist for decades.

What does this mean for Egypt's North Coast and Ras El Hekma?

They are being built at a scale beyond any resort in the benchmark, on a summer market. Heirstone's January Test sets five checks, from housing mix to winter air access and anchor institutions, for any coastal city plan.
