Branded Residences: Who keeps the premium

What a brand adds to a home after its fees and running costs, and where branded schemes fall short, with a view from the UAE, Egypt and Saudi Arabia.

In brief

The brand premium is banked at the first sale. The owner holds the part that has to last.

Branded homes do sell for more. For anyone underwriting one, the useful questions are how much of the extra price belongs to the brand, who receives it and whether it is still there when the owner sells. On the public evidence, most of it from Dubai, the brand's share is smaller than the headline numbers suggest, and most of it leaves the scheme at the first sale while the owner pays to keep the name on the building. The report then sets branded schemes in the UAE, Egypt and Saudi Arabia against six tests of whether a premium lasts.

  1. The headline premium mostly measures location.

    Published Dubai premiums run from 88.6% (Knight Frank, 2023) to 56% (Morgan's International Realty, H1 2026), on broad comparisons that include location. Matched to comparable homes, Savills found 29% in 2020 and Morgan's found under 10% in villa communities. The average off-plan branded price in H1 2025 sat within 0.2% of Knight Frank's Dubai Prime Index.

  2. Below a premium of about 20%, the brand does not pay for itself.

    A royalty of 5 to 6% of the sale price takes about a quarter of a 30% premium. Broker compilations put branded towers' service charges at 1.2 to 3.1 times their neighbours'. On our central case these two costs absorb the whole of a 19.7% premium.

  3. The developer and the brand are paid at signing. The owner carries the rest.

    Of a 30% premium, about 24% goes to the brand as a one-time fee and 76% to the developer, before higher build and sales costs. On our central case the owner nets about 4.4% against 5.3% for an unbranded home, and about 80% of Dubai's branded deals are off-plan first sales.

  4. Later trades fall back toward the building and its location.

    Ten registered trades at Bugatti Residences in August and September 2026 ranged from AED 3,105 to 11,259 per sq ft, with a median 38% below the AED 9,674 launch record. Armani Beach Residences traded at or just below its launch price two years on. At Bulgari Lighthouse, under 40 homes beside a Bulgari resort, a 2025 penthouse sale was 37% above the 2022 scheme average.

  5. Supply is running ahead of proof.

    Dubai's branded stock grew 34% in the year to June 2026 while branded sales fell 21%. Four-fifths of its pipeline has no hotel, according to Global Branded Residences. Licences also end: four of the five licensed hotel-condominium towers we traced in the Americas lost the name within about ten years.

  6. Outside Dubai, the premium has yet to be tested at resale.

    Cairo ranks fifth among Savills' ten largest branded residence cities, with 26 schemes in the pipeline against 2 completed, and Ras Al Khaimah has 24 against none. Outside Dubai we found only two published prices and no published service charge for any branded scheme.

What it means

Recommendations by audience

Land agencies and regulators

Publish branded resale data and require the brand's term, termination rights and service-charge budget to be disclosed at sale.

Developers

Underwrite a premium of about 30%, the level found against comparable homes, against the 20% bar, keep schemes small and phase homes to hotel delivery.

Investors and lenders

Value on registered trades and net yield, and price the chance that the name comes off.

Brands and operators

Write succession into the contract, ration the name by city and publish the running costs of each scheme.

Frequently asked questions

Do branded residences sell at a premium in Dubai?

Yes, but less than headline figures suggest. Broad comparisons put the Dubai premium at 56% to 88.6%, while against comparable homes Savills measured 29% in 2020 and Morgan's under 10% in villa communities. Much of the headline gap reflects location rather than the brand.

Who captures the branded residence premium?

Mostly the developer and the brand, at signing: of a 30% premium about 24% goes to the brand as a one-time fee and 76% to the developer. The owner earns about 1.5% a year on the extra capital and, on Heirstone's central case, needs a premium of about 20% just to cover the royalty and higher service charges.

Is there a branded residence resale market in Egypt and Saudi Arabia?

Not yet: Dubai is the only city in the region where Heirstone found registered branded trades. Cairo ranks fifth among Savills' ten largest branded residence cities with 26 schemes in the pipeline against 2 completed, and Saudi Arabia's foreign-ownership law took effect only in 2026.
